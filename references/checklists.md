# DossierLens checklists

Single source for every skill and the web app (generated from docs/checklists.js). Each item is scored **met**, **partly met** or **missing**.

## Faculty packet

**Reviewer role:** a senior faculty member on a tenure-track search committee at a land-grant university.

**Scores (1-10):** clarity; evidence (specific, quantified accomplishments); fit (alignment with this position and the job ad); feasibility (10 = realistic, achievable plans; low = overpromising); innovation (novelty and ambition of the proposed goals compared with the field); writing (grammar, concision, style).

### Research Statement

Typical length: 1200-2500 words (2–4 pages).

- **R1** Clearly state my long-term research vision and its significance
- **R2** Explain the research gap and why addressing it matters
- **R3** Present 2 to 4 interconnected research goals within a coherent narrative
- **R4** Connect short-term goals, achievable results, and preliminary data to the long-term vision in one cohesive paragraph rather than separate timelines
- **R5** Explain how early results will establish feasibility and support competitive grant proposals
- **R6** Connect each research goal to previous accomplishments and technical expertise
- **R7** Explain the novelty and scientific contribution of the proposed work
- **R8** Describe research questions, proposed methods, and expected outcomes
- **R9** Demonstrate research independence from my PhD advisor's work
- **R10** Identify realistic funding agencies and relevant programs
- **R11** Identify potential collaborators, facilities, and resources at the institution
- **R12** Explain how the advertised position and department's priorities overlap with my expertise and proposed research
- **R13** Describe anticipated research outputs, including publications, datasets, models, and technologies
- **R14** Explain broader scientific, economic, and societal impacts
- **R15** Describe opportunities for undergraduate and graduate research involvement
- **R16** Use relevant scholarly citations where appropriate

### Teaching Statement

Typical length: 700-1300 words (1–2 pages).

- **T1** Clearly explain my teaching philosophy and student learning goals
- **T2** Describe my actual teaching experience and responsibilities
- **T3** Provide concrete examples of teaching approaches I have used
- **T4** Explain how I will use hands-on, experiential, and project-based learning
- **T5** Show how I will integrate my interdisciplinary expertise and emerging technologies into teaching, where relevant to the course
- **T6** Identify existing courses I am qualified to teach
- **T7** Suggest potential new courses or modules that complement the department's curriculum
- **T8** Describe how I will assess student learning and provide constructive feedback
- **T9** Explain how I will support students with different academic backgrounds and levels of preparation
- **T10** Discuss undergraduate and graduate mentoring approaches
- **T11** Explain how I will improve teaching using feedback and learning outcomes
- **T12** Connect my teaching interests to the advertised position and department's needs

### Cover Letter

Typical length: 550-1100 words (1–2 pages). Also answer: **does it say why the applicant, rather than other applicants?**

- **C1** Use the correct position title, department, and university name
- **C2** Clearly state why I am applying and what attracts me to the position
- **C3** Summarize my academic qualifications and interdisciplinary expertise
- **C4** Highlight 2 to 3 strongest research accomplishments using concrete evidence and outcomes
- **C5** Briefly connect my future research direction to the position without duplicating the research statement
- **C6** Summarize my teaching qualifications and potential contributions without repeating the teaching statement
- **C7** Highlight Extension or outreach experience when relevant to the position
- **C8** Explain how my qualifications overlap with the specific requirements and priorities in the job advertisement
- **C9** Show knowledge of relevant institutional strengths and potential collaboration opportunities
- **C10** Communicate what distinguishes me as a candidate
- **C11** Maintain a concise, professional, and persuasive narrative
- **C12** Close with a clear expression of interest and appreciation

### Extension Philosophy

Typical length: 600-1300 words (1–2 pages).

- **E1** Explain my understanding of the land-grant Extension mission
- **E2** Identify the producers, industries, communities, or stakeholders I intend to serve
- **E3** Connect my expertise to the specific regional challenges relevant to the institution
- **E4** Explain how I will identify stakeholder needs through engagement and collaboration
- **E5** Describe how research findings can be translated into practical and affordable solutions
- **E6** Explain how I will validate technologies or recommendations in real-world settings
- **E7** Describe planned demonstrations, workshops, field days, or other Extension activities
- **E8** Explain how I will communicate technical findings to nontechnical audiences
- **E9** Address cost, usability, accessibility, and adoption barriers
- **E10** Describe potential partnerships with Extension specialists, agents, stakeholders, and industry
- **E11** Define measurable outcomes and methods for evaluating Extension impact
- **E12** Explain how stakeholder feedback will inform future research and Extension activities

### Outreach & Service

Typical length: 550-1200 words (1–2 pages).

- **O1** Explain my philosophy of academic service and public engagement
- **O2** Describe previous outreach and service activities with specific contributions
- **O3** Include relevant workshops, mentoring, and STEM engagement experience
- **O4** Highlight participation in hackathons, community programs, or interdisciplinary initiatives
- **O5** Describe completed professional service, including manuscript reviewing and conference involvement
- **O6** Distinguish completed service from planned future activities
- **O7** Explain potential contributions to departmental and university committees
- **O8** Discuss student organizations, recruitment, and mentoring
- **O9** Describe future K-12, community college, or public engagement activities
- **O10** Explain how my interdisciplinary expertise can contribute to outreach and professional service
- **O11** Connect proposed service activities to the institution's mission and needs
- **O12** Describe the intended impact on students, the profession, and communities

## Industry resume

**Reviewer role:** an experienced industry recruiter and hiring manager who also knows exactly how applicant tracking systems (ATS) such as Workday, Greenhouse, Lever and Taleo parse resumes.

**Scores (1-10):** clarity (easy to scan in 7 seconds); evidence (bullets quantify impact with numbers, %, $, scale); fit (keywords and skills match the job description truthfully); ats (will parse cleanly in an applicant tracking system); format (layout, consistency, length); writing (grammar, concision).

### Resume

Typical length: 350-900 words (1–2 pages).


**ATS-friendly**

- **A1** Single-column layout with no tables, text boxes, or side columns
- **A2** Standard section headings (Summary, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Publications)
- **A3** Contact details in the main body, not in the page header or footer
- **A4** No images, icons, logos, photos, or skill-rating bars
- **A5** Standard fonts and simple bullets; text is selectable and extracts cleanly
- **A6** Consistent date format for every role (e.g., Jan 2022 – Mar 2024)
- **A7** Job title, employer, location and dates on clear, parseable lines
- **A8** Uses the job description's exact skill and tool names where truthful
- **A9** Acronyms spelled out at least once (e.g., Machine Learning (ML))
- **A10** Skills listed as plain text keywords, grouped sensibly
- **A11** No keyword stuffing, hidden text, or white-font tricks
- **A12** Saved as .docx or a text-based PDF with a clear file name

**Strong resume format**

- **F1** Length fits experience: 1 page early career, 2 pages for PhD/experienced
- **F2** Targeted headline and 2–3 line summary for this role
- **F3** Reverse-chronological order within each section
- **F4** Every bullet starts with a strong action verb
- **F5** Bullets quantify impact (numbers, %, $, time saved, scale, users)
- **F6** Result-first bullets: accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z
- **F7** Academic work translated into industry terms (projects, outcomes, business value)
- **F8** Consistent tense: past for previous roles, present for the current role
- **F9** No personal pronouns, objective statement, photo, age or marital status
- **F10** Consistent alignment, spacing, bolding and punctuation
- **F11** LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio links written as plain URLs
- **F12** Most relevant experience and skills appear in the top third of page 1
- **F13** Outdated or irrelevant items removed

### Cover Letter

Typical length: 200-450 words (under 1 page). Also answer: **does it say why the applicant, rather than other applicants?**

- **L1** Names the exact role and company
- **L2** Opening hook that shows why this company and this role
- **L3** Two or three achievements that map to the job's top requirements
- **L4** Shows understanding of the company's product, users, or problem
- **L5** States clearly what distinguishes me from other applicants
- **L6** Translates research or academic experience into business value
- **L7** Concise (under ~400 words) and tailored, not a generic template
- **L8** Ends with a confident call to action

## Grant proposal

**Reviewer role:** an experienced grant review panelist and program officer who has scored many proposals for agencies such as NSF, NIH and USDA NIFA.

**Scores (1-10):** clarity (a reviewer outside the subfield can follow it); evidence (preliminary data, rigor, reproducibility); fit (alignment with the solicitation's priorities and review criteria); feasibility (scope realistic for budget, team and duration; low = overpromising); innovation (conceptual, methodological or technical novelty versus the state of the art); writing (grammar, concision).

### Project Narrative

Typical length: 3000-9000 words (per solicitation).

- **G1** Clear, specific central hypothesis or overall objective
- **G2** Significance and knowledge gap supported by recent citations
- **G3** Specific aims or objectives that are measurable and not dependent on each other succeeding
- **G4** Innovation stated explicitly: what is new versus the state of the art
- **G5** Preliminary data that demonstrate feasibility
- **G6** Approach per aim: design, methods, controls, sample size or power, analysis
- **G7** Expected outcomes and success metrics for each aim
- **G8** Potential pitfalls and alternative strategies
- **G9** Timeline and milestones realistic for budget and duration
- **G10** Team qualifications, roles, collaborators, and facilities
- **G11** Explicit alignment with the program's priorities and review criteria
- **G12** Broader impacts, societal relevance, or Extension/outreach
- **G13** Dissemination plan and outputs (papers, data, software, tools)
- **G14** Evaluation plan with measurable indicators
- **G15** Rigor and reproducibility (replication, blinding, relevant variables)
- **G16** Figures: a conceptual overview figure plus clear, labeled, referenced data figures
- **G17** Follows the solicitation's required sections, page limits, and formatting
- **G18** Readable, jargon-light prose with defined acronyms

### Summary / Specific Aims

Typical length: 250-700 words (1 page).

- **SA1** Opening sentence states the problem and why it matters
- **SA2** Knowledge gap and the critical need it creates
- **SA3** Long-term goal and the objective of this proposal
- **SA4** Central hypothesis and its basis (preliminary data)
- **SA5** 2–4 aims, each with a short title and approach
- **SA6** Expected outcomes and impact paragraph
- **SA7** Innovation is visible on this page
- **SA8** Fits the page or character limit

### Budget Justification

Typical length: 300-1500 words (per solicitation).

- **B1** Every budget line is justified by a specific activity in the narrative
- **B2** Personnel effort matches the roles described
- **B3** Equipment and supplies are itemized with need explained
- **B4** Travel is tied to dissemination or fieldwork
- **B5** Costs follow agency rules (indirect costs, allowable items, cost share)
- **B6** Totals are consistent with the budget forms

### Data Management Plan

Typical length: 300-1200 words (1–2 pages).

- **D1** Types and volume of data, samples, software and other products
- **D2** Standards and formats for data and metadata
- **D3** Storage, backup and security during the project
- **D4** Public access and sharing: repository, timing and license
- **D5** Privacy, ethics, or intellectual property constraints addressed
- **D6** Roles, responsibilities, and long-term preservation

## Where to find funded proposals

- [NIAID sample applications](https://www.niaid.nih.gov/grants-contracts/sample-applications): Full funded NIH R01/R03/R21 applications with summary statements.
- [NIDCD sample grant applications](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/funding/sample-grant-applications): Funded NIH applications in hearing, voice and communication research.
- [Open Grants](https://www.ogrants.org): Proposals shared by researchers across fields and agencies, funded and not.
- [NSF Award Search](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/): Abstracts of funded NSF awards; filter by program and year.
- [NIH RePORTER](https://reporter.nih.gov): Abstracts and aims of funded NIH projects; filter by fiscal year.
- [USDA NIFA Data Gateway](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/data/data-gateway): Funded NIFA projects with objectives and methods summaries.
